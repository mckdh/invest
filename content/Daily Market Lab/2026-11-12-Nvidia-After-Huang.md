# Nvidia After Huang: 7-Lens Read (Nov 12, 2026) 📉

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on Thursday, and I used the news as a practice round for my seven-lens market routine. First, I forced myself to write down my own gut read before looking up the data. Then I checked what the market actually did and graded where I got it wrong. I am keeping this as a personal study log to sharpen my thinking over time.

## 📰 The News

Nvidia's board announced that Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO, and board member, effective immediately. News reports say he admitted to three extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly suspected of being a Chinese spy (that spy angle is alleged, not proven). CFO Colette Kress is stepping in as interim CEO, and Congress is reportedly asking for answers.

**How the market reacted**
- NVDA dropped as much as roughly 13% early in the session, bounced back, and closed at $166.19, down 4.34%.
- The S&P 500 and Dow each slipped around 0.4%, and the Nasdaq 100 dipped about 0.3%.
- After hours, futures edged higher: S&P 500 futures rose about 0.2% (near 7,630) and Nasdaq 100 futures added about 0.3% (near 28,470).

## 🔎 What the Data Showed

**Policy and geopolitics:** My export-control instinct had some smoke behind it. Super Micro Computer fell roughly 11% amid a probe into whether companies tied to the alleged spy smuggled chips. Still, right now that is an investigation and political pressure from Congress, not a signed export ban.

**Momentum and institutional flows:** The competitor angle held up well. Ahead of next week's earnings, institutions reportedly trimmed heavy Nvidia positions and rotated into rivals instead of leaving tech altogether. AMD and Broadcom climbed, and Qualcomm jumped. Money stayed inside the AI trade and simply shifted seats.

**Liquidity:** Conditions look tight. Fed net liquidity sat around $5.79 trillion on November 4, down roughly $74 billion over four weeks. Meanwhile, the Treasury's checking account (TGA) climbed to about $957 billion, which pulls cash out of the financial system, and the overnight reverse repo cushion (RRP) is basically empty. I could not verify a fresh money market fund number, so I cannot claim sideline cash is rushing back into stocks yet.

**Macro:** Bond yields moved up and oil price pressure stayed in focus. I did not dig far enough here, so this lens stays incomplete.

**Sentiment:** Retail mood on SPY, QQQ, and DIA flipped from bearish in late October to extremely bullish, and Fundstrat's Tom Lee also called a bottom in tech last week. When everyone crowds onto the same optimistic side of the boat, it often works as a gentle warning sign.

**Chart:** NVDA is down about 27.6% over the past month from its 52-week high of $243.37. That matters because the drop started *before* Huang's resignation, driven by AI-capex and debt worries. The QQQ and SOXX support levels I pulled from articles looked mixed and stale, so I want to draw my own zones on TradingView before relying on them.

## ✅ Grading My Own Analysis

**What worked:** Policy, geopolitics, and competitor rotation all pointed in the right direction. Connecting a CEO scandal to China export scrutiny made sense because Nvidia's China business is already under a political microscope.

**Where I missed:**
- Thinking "chips staying in the U.S. will lower costs" was backwards. U.S. AI companies depend on Nvidia GPUs as a core input, so a supply or governance scare usually tightens supply and pushes costs up, rather than making chips cheaper.
- A CEO headline rarely triggers a new government rule overnight. Export controls go through slow national security reviews, so expecting an immediate policy ban was jumping the gun.
- Blaming Thursday's drop solely on Huang's exit ignores that NVDA had already been sliding for weeks.

**Another angle to remember:** Think of this like a sudden leadership discount, similar to Intel in 2018. The real test over the next few weeks is whether the drag stays on NVDA alone or spills into the rest of the chip basket (SOXX). Even without a single new law passed, noisy congressional hearings can still shake the stock on headlines alone.

## 🔭 Today's Read and Tomorrow's Watchlist

Overall, this looked like a contained shake-up with rotation inside tech, rather than a market-wide panic. The broad indexes barely lost half a percent, and futures ticked up after the bell.

Things I am watching next:
- Next week's Nvidia earnings call, and any hint about a permanent CEO
- Whether MNQ and SOXX hold Thursday's lows
- Actual export-control policy moves versus noisy headlines
- Whether money keeps rotating into AMD, Broadcom, and Qualcomm
